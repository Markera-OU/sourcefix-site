03 / HOW IT WORKS

Publish. Review. Done.

It runs at publish time, inside WordPress. There is nothing to remember and no separate tool to open.

01 / SCAN

Publish a post

SourceFix scans it for missing alt text, vague link text, and heading problems. The scan takes under a second.

02 / REVIEW

Approve the fixes

Each proposal sits in an approval queue. Edit the wording, approve it, or skip it. Nothing changes until you approve.

03 / APPLY

It is logged

Approved fixes go live. Every fix is recorded with the date and who approved it, and your public statement page is updated.

04 / WHAT IT FIXES

Three problems. Every post.

The ones that show up on almost every WordPress site.

Alt text, written for you

SourceFix looks at each image and writes a plain description of what it shows. Decorative images get an empty alt, so screen readers skip them.

Link text that says where it goes

“Click here” becomes “Read the pricing page”. A link makes sense on its own, out of context.

Headings in order

SourceFix flags skipped levels and a missing H1, and suggests a clean order so the page reads top to bottom.

An approval queue

Nothing changes on your site until you approve it. On paid plans, low risk fixes can apply themselves.

A public statement

A page on your site lists what SourceFix does and what it has fixed, with dates. It is re-published whenever the fixes change.

An audit log

Every fix, who approved it, and when. 30 days on the free plan, 12 months on paid plans.

06 / QUESTIONS

Asked, answered.

Does SourceFix make my site compliant?No. It fixes specific issues: alt text, link text, headings. Compliance is a legal question, and SourceFix does not claim it.

What does it check?Alt text on images, the wording of links, and the order of headings. That is all it does, and it does it at publish time.

Do I have to approve every fix?On the free plan, yes. Paid plans can auto-apply low risk fixes. You can turn that off.

What WordPress versions does it need?WordPress 6.0 or newer, PHP 7.4 or newer.
